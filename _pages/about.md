Hi, welcome to my personal website! My name is Xinzhi Rao (饶莘植, pronounced "Sheen-jr Rao"). I am a Ph.D. candidate at the [Warrington College of Business](https://warrington.ufl.edu/), University of Florida, advised by [Professor Liangfei Qiu](https://sites.google.com/site/qiuliangfei), [Professor Jingchuan Pu](https://warrington.ufl.edu/directory/jingchuan-pu/), and [Professor Anuj Kumar](https://warrington.ufl.edu/directory/anuj-kumar/).

My research interest sits at the intersection of information systems, digital platform strategy, and the economics of artificial intelligence. My research focuses on three main streams: (1) **complementor strategies and platform competition**, where I leverage a seven-year panel of content creators and proprietary seller data to examine how complementors' strategic choices — such as multihoming across content platforms or wholesaling to Amazon — reshape their own performance and the platforms they serve; (2) **the economics of generative AI**, where I study how generative AI transforms what users contribute on knowledge platforms and how faithfully LLM-generated summaries convey user opinions to consumers; and (3) **information disclosure and platform design**, where I examine how platforms' information provision — from introductory sessions on online education platforms to security advisories on GitHub — shapes participation, engagement, and market outcomes.

Methodologically, my work combines causal inference on large-scale observational data, including difference-in-differences designs built on natural experiments and staggered adoption, with analytical modeling and randomized and survey experiments. I also adapt machine learning, deep learning, and large language model tools for empirical research. My work is forthcoming at *MIS Quarterly*.

I am on the 2026–27 academic job market.
